### 2. Key Findings
- **Excessive UDP Listening Ports:** 37 UDP endpoints are actively listening, which is unusually high for a typical system and expands the attack surface.
- **Suspicious Wildcard Bindings:** Multiple services are bound to `0.0.0.0` (all interfaces), including ports 49666, 49665, 49664, 49678, 49674, 49669, and 5040. This makes services accessible from any network interface, increasing exposure risk.
- **Use of Well-Known/Sensitive Ports:**
  - Port **139** (NetBIOS Session Service) is open on `172.17.96.1` and `192.168.0.105`. This protocol is often targeted for SMB-related attacks.
  - Port **445** (Microsoft-DS) is open on `0.0.0.0`, which is commonly exploited (e.g., EternalBlue vulnerabilities).
  - Port **135** (RPC Endpoint Mapper) is open on multiple interfaces, potentially exposing remote procedure call services.
- **Localhost Exposure:** Several services (e.g., ports 5000, 4096, 8000) are bound to `127.0.0.1`, which is expected, but combined with external interfaces, this creates complex exposure scenarios.
- **Port Reuse:** Port `5000` appears twice in the listening list (on `127.0.0.1` and `0.0.0.0`), which may indicate misconfigured duplicate services.
- **Unusual High Port Usage:** Ports like `49666`, `49665`, `49664`, etc., are dynamically assigned but may indicate custom or suspicious applications masquerading as ephemeral services.

### 3. Recommendations
- **Minimize Listening Surface:** Disable or reconfigure unnecessary UDP services. Review each listening endpoint
